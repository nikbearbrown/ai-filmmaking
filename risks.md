## Comparable Texts Analysis

**1. Blain Brown, *The Basics of Filmmaking: Screenwriting, Producing, Directing, Cinematography, Audio, Editing* (Routledge, 2020)**
- *Reader:* introductory film production students.
- *Strongest:* department-by-department coverage of a real production, with forms, terminology and safety.
- *Weakest:* no AI; assumes a crew, a set and a budget.
- *Why a filmmaker might choose it:* an established, thorough reference (many trained filmmakers already own one like it).
- *Why choose this course:* it carries the same departments' craft to one person directing an agent.

**2. Tom Barrance, *Start Making Movies* (Learnaboutfilm, 2021)**
- *Reader:* beginners and schools.
- *Strongest:* a plain step-by-step route: story, picture, sound, sequence, time, essential scenes, editing and sharing.
- *Weakest:* no AI; the depth stops at the beginner level.
- *Why choose it:* short and approachable.
- *Why choose this course:* a trained filmmaker is past the beginner route; this course starts from their screenplay.

**3. Kelly Gordon Brine, *The Art of Cinematic Storytelling* (Oxford University Press, 2020)**
- *Reader:* directing and storyboarding students.
- *Strongest:* the deepest shot and cut grammar in the pantry, with ready-made recipes for dialogue, chases, crowds and driving scenes.
- *Weakest:* no producing, no sound, no AI.
- *Why choose it:* for shot design alone.
- *Why choose this course:* it takes shot design the filmmaker already has and gets a model to produce it, inside a whole pipeline from screenplay to release.

**4. Curious Refuge, *AI Filmmaking Course* (online, four weeks; syllabus in the pantry, undated)**
- *Reader:* creatives who want AI tools quickly.
- *Strongest:* broad tool coverage with a project every week.
- *Weakest:* starts from zero; little craft; tied to specific tools; its week 2 goals include removing watermarks from videos.
- *Why choose it:* short and practical.
- *Why choose this course:* it starts from the filmmaker's own screenplay. It gives them an agent they direct, honest disclosure, and no creative calls made for them.

**5. James Hutson and Andrew Allen Smith, *Cinematic Algorithms: The Rise of Generative AI in Video Art and Visual Culture* (CRC Press, 2025, 1st edition)**
- *Reader:* students and scholars of media, art and AI.
- *Strongest:* history, ethics and authorship, and 12 interviews with AI filmmakers.
- *Weakest:* a tool chapter that dated within a year; no exercises; no cost.
- *Why choose it:* to study AI and visual culture.
- *Why choose this course:* to make a film from a screenplay.

## Differentiation Statements

- *The Basics of Filmmaking* teaches a crewed production by department. This course carries the same craft to one filmmaker directing an agent, for trained filmmakers with a screenplay and no crew.
- *Start Making Movies* teaches beginners. This course starts where a trained filmmaker already is, with a finished screenplay.
- *The Art of Cinematic Storytelling* teaches shot design in depth. This course gets a model to produce the shots the filmmaker designs, for filmmakers who want to finish a film.
- The Curious Refuge course teaches tools week by week, from zero. This course starts from the screenplay and keeps tools in dated boxes, for filmmakers who want skills that survive the next model release.
- *Cinematic Algorithms* surveys AI in film. This course produces a film with every cost and failure on the record, for filmmakers who want to make work, not study it.

## Adoption Risk Register

**Field stability** (Content)
- *Likelihood:* High; the pantry's 2025 tool chapter already names tools Bear's 2026 runs did not use.
- *Impact:* High.
- *Trigger:* a named model, price, or "the model follows this word" claim in a chapter is wrong at publication.
- *Mitigation:* tools, prices and test results only in dated boxes; defaults, overrides and judgment in the running text.
- *Contingency:* refresh the boxes each edition.

**Learner cost** (Market)
- *Likelihood:* High. *Impact:* High.
- *Trigger:* filmmakers cannot finish Part III for lack of credits.
- *Mitigation:* the cost order and the two still tiers (most exercises run on draft stills); a test budget in Ch 7; a free or low-cost path.
- *Contingency:* a stills-only route through the course, with motion optional. [NEEDS HUMAN INPUT — who pays]

**Self-paced drop-off** (Structure)
- *Likelihood:* Medium to High. *Impact:* High.
- *Trigger:* filmmakers stop at the gate between stills and motion (Ch 7), where the spending starts.
- *Mitigation:* the pebble in the Introduction gives an early finished film; every chapter ends in a file; the capstone is the filmmaker's own scene.
- *Contingency:* make the capstone possible as a reviewed storyboard with only a few clips.

**Re-teaching craft** (Content, new 2026-11-17)
- *Likelihood:* Medium. *Impact:* High for this reader.
- *Trigger:* a chapter explains what a close-up or a key light is.
- *Mitigation:* every chapter opens with "from your craft to the model", which assumes the craft; *Agentic Filmmaking* takes the teaching.
- *Contingency:* move the passage to *Agentic Filmmaking*.

## Top 3 Adoption Risks

**1. Filmmakers cannot afford the expensive steps.** Motion is where the money goes. The filmmaker this course is written for has a screenplay and no budget; Galen has spent most of his time raising money. The cost order and the two still tiers help, because most of the work happens on cheap draft stills. But the course still needs a decided answer to who pays and a stills-first route, or it excludes its own audience.

**2. The AI half ages faster than the course ships.** Every AI source in the pantry was out of step with Bear's own tools within a year. Which words a model follows ages fastest of all. If tool names, prices or test results leak into the running text, the course looks stale on day one. The anatomy keeps them in dated boxes. The discipline is holding that line in every chapter.

**3. Consent, rights, and the creative calls.** The running case is a real person's story and characters. The exercises involve likeness, voices and music. The thesis promises that the course never makes the creative calls. Any one of these would damage the course's central claim:
- a withdrawn consent;
- a rights dispute;
- a chapter that decides for the filmmaker.

The defenses are written consent from Galen, rights records in every exercise, and sessions Galen runs himself.
